Section 01 / What I Thought It Was

I thought electricity was the job. It wasn't.

Blake, 19, commercial electrician apprentice, Atlanta. First two weeks I moved conduit. Hundreds of feet of it. Up stairs, across floors, into gangboxes. Nobody handed me a wire. I thought I'd be learning the trade. I was learning whether I'd show up.

Section 02 / What Surprised Me

The journeyman didn't teach me. He tested me. Every task was a filter. Could I take direction without asking why? Could I find the gangbox without being shown twice? The teaching came later — when I proved I'd stay.

Section 03 / The Cost of Failure

A mistake here isn't a bug report. It's a crushed hand, a ruined shift, or worse.

I cross-threaded a coupling on my third week. Small mistake. The journeyman didn't yell. He made me take it apart and redo it. Twice. In front of the crew. That's how you learn what careful means.

Section 05 / The Lesson at 18

Your body is the real tool.

If you lift with your back because you want to look tough in front of the crew, you'll be sitting in a truck by 28 while someone else does the real work. Learn to use the hoist. Protect your knees before they ask for it.

One voice. One trade. One reality.
